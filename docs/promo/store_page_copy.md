## Full description

**One Man Remains**

A longship left the settlement five months ago on a trading and exploration voyage. It was expected back by midsummer. It drifts into the fjord unannounced, oars shipped, no one steering.

Of the twenty-three people who left, one is still alive: Hakon, found unconscious beneath the sailcloth. He knows every face in the settlement. He remembers the first night of the voyage. Past that — nothing. He says, calmly and often, that they never left the fjord at all.

The ship says otherwise. Foreign cargo. Worn gear that traveled somewhere real. Salt worked into hands that "never left."

You knew these people. You're close enough to the situation to start asking questions nobody else wants to ask yet — and this short first chapter follows that investigation through three threads: the objects recovered from the ship, the settlement's witnesses (each holding a different piece and a different grief), and Hakon's own memory, surfacing in fragments.

This is a grounded Viking Age story, not a monster-hunt. Whatever actually happened out there is left exactly as uncertain as real belief and rumor would leave it — no confirmed creature, no tidy mundane explanation. The question the game actually asks isn't "where did they go." It's "why were they afraid to come home."

**What this is:**
- A short, self-contained first chapter (roughly 15-30 minutes) of a longer planned exploration/story RPG.
- Grid-based top-down movement and dialogue in the spirit of GBA-era Pokemon — but built around exploration, conversation, and light tension rather than battling or collecting.
- A small, real cast, each with their own stake in what happened.
- Item-based progression, hidden secrets for the curious, and a couple of quiet "hold your nerve" moments instead of combat.

**What this isn't (yet):**
- This is an early public build, not a finished game. Art is programmatically generated placeholder art (a real art bible and hand-tuned pipeline are in progress) rather than final illustration.
- It's intentionally short. This chapter proves the world and the systems; a longer game is being built in acts on top of it.

Free. Runs in your browser, no download.
